Honors thesis

How does college shape a career?

Advised by Professor Warren Lowell, I'm studying how residential mobility, social capital, and nonlinear paths shape graduates' career success. It's the same question a recruiter asks every day: what really drives someone's trajectory?

Read the pilot study

“For these alumni, who to trust mattered just as much as where to apply.”

From my pilot study

20
semi-structured alumni interviews
78
HOD alumni personally recruited
35
analytic categories coded in MAXQDA
4
themes synthesized from the data

Four early findings from my pilot study, with the full thesis now in progress:

  • Career paths are nonlinear.
  • Relocation is relational rather than economic.
  • Social capital actively redirected career opportunities.
  • Structural factors shape which opportunities feel possible, influencing major choice, emotional adjustment, and workplace experiences.
